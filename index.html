10–14 · 77–78 7 topics
10 Circuit Breakers Stops calling a failing dependency: fail fast, then half-open to probe for recovery. Payment provider starts 500-ing; breaker opens 30 s and checkout degrades to “pay later”. ▶Circuit breaker in microservices — sudoCODE12 min ⌘Resilience4j MIT 6.033Computer System Engineering
11 Timeouts A hard deadline on every network call, ideally propagated as a shrinking budget. 2 s gRPC deadline at the edge; downstream calls inherit 1.5 s so work stops when the caller gives up. ▶Timeouts, retries, backoff with jitter — MsDeep Singh27 min ⌘Toxiproxy (fault injection) MIT 6.033Computer System Engineering
12 Retries Re-attempts transient failures only: idempotent operations, capped attempts, jittered waits. Retry a 503 twice with jitter; never retry a 400 or a non-idempotent POST. ▶Retry patterns explained — Systemic Stack16 min ⌘Toxiproxy (fault injection) MIT 6.033Computer System Engineering
13 Exponential Backoff Waits 1 s, 2 s, 4 s, 8 s… between attempts, with jitter to break synchronization. Client backs off with full jitter after a 429; five attempts max, then surface a real error. ▶Retries and exponential backoff — glich.stream31 min ⌘Toxiproxy (fault injection) MIT 6.033Computer System Engineering
14 Idempotency Guarantees repeating the same request has the same effect as doing it once. Stripe-style Idempotency-Key header; INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING for dedupe. ▶Idempotency in APIs — Arkynate6 min ⌘System Design Primer MIT 6.033Computer System Engineering
77 Network Partitions A split where nodes are alive but cannot reach each other, forcing a consistency-vs-availability choice. 78 Clock Skew Machines disagree about wall-clock time, which breaks cross-node ordering assumptions. If you only do six things

  1. Foundations — Latency, throughput, percentiles, timeouts, retries, idempotency, caching. These explain most real production bugs. (73–76 · 11–14 · 2 · 47)
  2. Make one service survive — Circuit breakers, backpressure, metrics, logs, traces, health probes. (10 · 41 · 55–58 · 53–54)
  3. Ship it — Containers, pipelines, deploy strategies, feature flags. (7 · 6 · 49–52 · 48)
  4. Scale the data — Indexes first, then replicas, then partitioning, only then sharding. (25 → 26 → 28 → 27)
  5. Go distributed — Queues, pub/sub, sagas, DLQs, leader election, locks, CAP. (15–20 · 30 · 35 · 31–32)
  6. Harden and operate — Secrets, IAM, OAuth, TLS, WAF, SLOs, incidents, postmortems. (64–72 · 60–63 · 91–93)
